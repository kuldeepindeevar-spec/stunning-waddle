The two hypotheses

H1 · Informational edge

Pagaya's model prices the reject pile better than the partners' models because it sees cross-partner data they cannot. If true, the edge compounds with volume, losses fall at constant approval rates, spreads tighten against comparable consumer ABS, and a network multiple is justified.

H2 · Regulatory conduit

The reject pile is profitable mainly because bank capital rules and credit-policy constraints force partners to decline loans that are economically sound. The edge is real but rented — it belongs to the rulebook, not the model — it erodes as underwriting AI diffuses to the partners themselves, and it deserves a specialty-finance multiple.

Three variables moved together over the disclosed period — model quality, tightened underwriting thresholds, and asset mix — so no single-quarter improvement in losses can be attributed to any one of them. Only the pre-committed tests below discriminate.

Panel 2The truth clock

Fees are booked in the quarter. The truth about the loans arrives over three to five years, inside unconsolidated trusts this company does not report on. Any business with front-loaded fees and back-loaded losses looks brilliant while volume grows, because new fees outrun old losses. The model is only tested when growth stops.
